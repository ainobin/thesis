# Core Architecture & Methodology

The system is designed for an active factory production line to grade bricks automatically at a speed of 2 to 4 bricks per second.

## 1. Hardware Integration

The setup uses low-cost components to build an automated sorting loop:

- **CMOS Web-Camera & Lighting Box:** A camera inside a closed box with a constant light source. Constant lighting is crucial because external shadows or color shifts skew image matrix values.
- **Sensors & Interfacing:** Object sensors detect when a brick passes on the conveyor belt, triggering the camera. A LabJack interfacing device and control card manage inputs and send trigger signals to a mechanical sorting hand to physically push rejected bricks away.

## 2. The DSP / Image Processing Pipeline

The software, built in MATLAB, processes the brick's physical profile through these steps:

- **Binarization:** Converts the raw RGB image into a binary image (black and white) and runs basic noise removal filters.
- **Geometric Extractions:**
  - **Area:** Calculated by summing up all pixels with a binary value of 1.
  - **Perimeter:** Determined by detecting the boundary edges and calculating the sum of the edge pixels.
  - **Diameter 1:** Calculated as the maximum distance between any two farthest points on the edge matrix.
  - **Diameter 2:** Computed by removing a 20% slice of pixels from both ends of Diameter 1 (40% total spatial reduction) and calculating the remaining inner cross-width profile.
  - **Cracks:** Detected by tracing the boundaries of internal holes or unexpected open spaces inside the brick matrix.

## Mathematical Ratios & Ideal Dimensions

The algorithm compares real-world brick measurements against an ideal brick size ($24 \times 12 \times 8 \text{ cm}$). Mathematically, an ideal top-down view forms a perfect $2X \times X$ rectangle. The authors derived four fixed geometric ratios to identify structural deviations:

$$Ratio_1 = \frac{\text{Diameter}_1^2}{\text{Area}} = \frac{5X^2}{2X^2} = 2.5 \text{ (Ideal Value)}$$

$$Ratio_2 = \frac{\text{Perimeter}}{\text{Diameter}_1} = \frac{6X}{X\sqrt{5}} = 2.68 \text{ (Ideal Value)}$$

$$Ratio_3 = \frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Diameter}_2 \times \text{Diameter}_1} = \frac{2X^2}{(0.6X)(X\sqrt{5})} = 1.49 \text{ (Ideal Value)}$$

$$Ratio_4 = \frac{\text{Perimeter}}{\text{Diameter}_2} = \frac{6X}{0.6X} = 10 \text{ (Ideal Value)}$$

> **The Sorting Rule:** If any computed ratio deviates even slightly from these ideal values, the brick is flagged as defective and rejected on the conveyor line.
