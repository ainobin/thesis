# Detection and Classification of Obstructive Sleep Apnea Using Audio Spectrogram Analysis (Serrano et al., 2024)

This paper applies transfer learning from pre-trained audio models (VGGish) combined with a bidirectional LSTM to classify obstructive sleep apnea events from audio spectrograms — directly demonstrating how pre-trained convolutional front-ends can be repurposed for acoustic classification tasks.

---

## 1. Core Problem & Dataset

- **Obstructive Sleep Apnea–Hypopnea Syndrome (OSAHS):** A sleep disorder caused by upper airway constriction. Diagnosis traditionally requires polysomnography in a clinical lab setting.
- **Dataset:** 212 full-night polysomnograms with synchronized high-quality tracheal and ambient microphone recordings, manually annotated by medical experts into apnea events (Obstructive, Central, Mixed, Hypopnea) and No-Apnea states.
- **Goal:** Continuous, automatic detection of apnea events from raw audio without explicit snore segmentation.

---

## 2. Preprocessing & Feature Pipeline

- **Mel-Spectrogram Generation:** Raw audio was transformed into mel-spectrograms using standard STFT parameters, capturing time-frequency energy distributions.
- **VGGish Front-End:** The convolutional stages of VGGish (up to the "pool4" layer) were used as a fixed feature extractor. VGGish was pre-trained on AudioSet (a massive dataset of 527 audio classes), providing a robust general-purpose audio feature embedding.
- **Sequence Construction:** Consecutive mel-spectrogram frames were stacked into fixed-length sequences to capture temporal context.

---

## 3. Hybrid Architecture: VGGish + bi-LSTM

- **VGGish Submodel (Frozen):** The convolutional layers of VGGish up to pool4 produce a compact feature vector per time segment. These layers are **not fine-tuned** — they act as a fixed, generic audio encoder.
- **bi-LSTM Classifier:** On top of the frozen VGGish embeddings, a bidirectional LSTM network processes the temporal sequence to model long-range dependencies in the audio signal.
- **Binary Output:** The final layer classifies each sequence as "Apnea" or "No-Apnea."

> **Key Design Choice:** Freezing the VGGish front-end prevents overfitting on the relatively small medical dataset while leveraging the rich representations learned from AudioSet's diverse audio corpus.

---

## 4. Key Findings & Relevance to Your Thesis

### Transfer Learning from General Audio to Domain-Specific Tasks

The VGGish model, originally trained for general audio tagging, successfully transferred to the highly specialized medical domain of sleep apnea detection. This validates that pre-trained convolutional audio embeddings can serve as universal feature extractors — exactly the paradigm you would use to adapt generic sound classification architectures to brick grading.

### Continuous vs. Segmented Classification

Rather than detecting and segmenting individual snore events, the system operates on continuous audio streams. This mirrors your brick inspection pipeline where audio is captured continuously on a production line and must be classified without explicit event segmentation.

### Smartphone-Grade Microphone Viability

The authors argue that modern smartphone MEMS microphones have comparable specifications to professional measurement microphones, suggesting that high-quality acoustic classification is feasible with low-cost, readily available hardware — supporting your use of affordable recording equipment for industrial brick inspection.

When your defense panel questions whether pre-trained CNN models can generalize from general audio domains (music, speech) to the specialized domain of brick acoustics, this paper provides a concrete precedent: VGGish + bi-LSTM successfully made a similar cross-domain leap into medical audio diagnosis, achieving clinically relevant accuracy without training the convolutional backbone from scratch.
