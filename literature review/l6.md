# Audio Recognition using Mel Spectrograms and Convolution Neural Networks (Zhang, Leitner & Thornton, 2019)

A systematic comparison of custom CNN architectures versus transfer learning (VGG19) for multi-label audio classification, demonstrating that a well-tuned compact CNN can outperform large pre-trained models when training data is limited — directly supporting the architectural choices in your brick grading pipeline.

---

## 1. Problem & Dataset

- **Task:** Multi-label audio classification across 80 sound categories using the Freesound Dataset (FSD) from the Kaggle Freesound Audio Tagging 2019 challenge.
- **Challenge:** Limited training data relative to the number of classes and the high intra-class variability of environmental sounds.
- **Preprocessing:** Raw audio was converted to mel-spectrograms to leverage well-established image classification techniques on sound data.

---

## 2. Three Model Architectures Compared

### Model A: Custom Deep CNN

- A relatively compact architecture designed from scratch through iterative hyperparameter tuning.
- Small enough to train fully on the available data without massive overfitting.
- Achieved the **best overall performance**.

### Model B: VGG19 Transfer Learning

- Pre-trained VGG19 (ImageNet weights) with the last convolution block and fully connected layers retrained.
- Hypothesis: Lower-level visual features (edges, textures) from ImageNet transfer to spectrogram patterns.
- Significantly **faster training** than the custom CNN.

### Model C: VGG19 From Scratch

- The same VGG19 architecture trained entirely without pre-trained weights.
- Expected to perform worst due to the large capacity vs. limited data mismatch.

---

## 3. Performance Results

| Model | Epochs | LWLRAP | Top-5 Accuracy |
|-------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Custom Deep CNN | 400 | **0.813** | **88.9%** |
| VGG19 Transfer Learning | 100 | 0.797 | 88.5% |
| VGG19 From Scratch | 400 | 0.737 | 82.9% |

- **Custom CNN won:** A purpose-built, smaller architecture generalized better than the massive VGG19 when data was scarce. The authors balanced complexity against overtraining through careful hyperparameter iteration.
- **Transfer learning was efficient:** It achieved near-matching accuracy with only 100 epochs (vs. 400), confirming that pre-trained convolution layers provide useful generic feature extractors.
- **Scratch training failed:** Large networks without pre-training cannot learn meaningful representations from small datasets — they memorize the training set and fail to generalize.

---

## 4. Class-Level Analysis

- **Best-classified sounds:** Musical instruments (bass drum, harmonica, hi-hat, finger snapping) — these have well-defined, consistent frequency signatures that mel-spectrograms capture cleanly.
- **Worst-classified sounds:** Sounds with high variability and confusability (walking, squeaking, water-related sounds) — these overlap in time-frequency space even for human listeners.

## 5. Relevance to Your Thesis

### Compact Architecture Justification

Your CNN (110K parameters, 3 conv layers) mirrors the design philosophy of this paper's Custom Deep CNN: a compact network tailored to the dataset size. When your examiners ask why you did not use a deeper network like ResNet or VGG, you can point to this paper as evidence that smaller, purpose-built CNNs outperform large pre-trained models when the training set is limited (30 samples in your case vs. 80 classes in theirs).

### Mel-Spectrogram as the Universal Front-End

Both your pipeline and this paper use mel-spectrograms as the input representation, confirming that mel-scale compression is a well-established preprocessing step that preserves discriminative acoustic information while reducing dimensionality.

### Transfer Learning as a Viable Alternative (With Caveats)

The paper's transfer learning result shows that pre-trained image networks can be adapted to spectrograms with strong efficiency gains. However, the custom CNN still won on accuracy — supporting your decision to build and train a dedicated architecture rather than relying solely on transfer learning.
